The Eye of Ra

4 min read June 23

In the time before time had a name, Ra sailed across the sky in his golden barque, the sun blazing from his crown like a wound in the heavens.

One morning, he woke to find his two children gone — Shu and Tefnut, wind and moisture, had wandered into the dark primordial waters and been lost.

Ra reached into his own face and pulled out his eye, sending it spinning into the black to find them. The eye obeyed. It searched the deep places, the cold corridors between stars, and finally — after an age — returned with his children at its side.

But Ra had not waited. In the eye's absence, he had grown another. And when his first eye returned to find itself replaced, it wept.

Ra, moved by its grief, placed that first eye upon his forehead, where it became the uraeus — the cobra that guards all pharaohs. A thing of fury and love, forever watching.
